# Bakers Dozen

A one-deck game with no stock and every card face up from the start, so nothing is hidden and it comes down to planning rather than luck. Some deals can't be won, but most reward a careful look before you move.

## The deal

One standard 52-card deck is dealt out completely into thirteen columns of four cards, all face up. Then any Kings in a column are moved to the bottom of it, underneath the other cards, so a King never sits on top of a lower card.

There is no stock and no waste. The four foundations start empty.

## Goal

Build all 52 cards onto the four foundations, one suit to each, from Ace up to King.

## Moves

- Only one card moves at a time, and it's always the top card of a pile.
- A card can go onto a column whose top card is one rank higher, in any suit: any 7 goes on any 8.
- An empty column can't be refilled. Once a column is cleared it stays empty for the rest of the game.
- Foundations build up by suit from Ace to King. Double-click a column (or press f on it) to send its top card to its foundation.
- Cards on the foundations are still in play. The top card of a foundation can be moved back onto a column whose top card is one rank higher. It can't be moved to another foundation.
- There's no stock, so there's nothing to deal and no redeals.

Autoplay only runs when you ask for it, and it's careful. Aces and twos always go up when they can, but a higher card stays put while any card one rank below it (of any suit) is still in the columns, because that card might want to build on it.

Once every card left can go up, a sends them all up as one move, and the message line says when that is.

## Scoring

You get 1 point for each card you put on a foundation, and lose it again if that card comes back down, so the score is always the number of cards on the foundations. A won game scores 52. That's AisleRiot's scoring too: one point a card, 52 at most.

## Options

Bakers Dozen has no options, so every deal is played by the rules above.

## Tips

- Nothing is hidden, so before your first move find the Aces and twos and see how many cards are sitting on each. The ones buried deepest need work first.
- A King can only leave its column by going to a foundation, so a column with a King at the bottom can't be emptied until late in the game. Once the cards above that King are gone, it's a safe place to build Queen, Jack, 10 and down: a buried King never holds up any other card.
- Every column top is somewhere to put a card, and a column you clear is lost for good. Don't empty one just because you can.
- Because foundation cards can come back, a foundation can lend you a spare spot. Say the 4 of hearts is sitting on the 3 of hearts you need next, and there's a 6 on top of a column but no 5. Bring a 5 of another suit down from its foundation onto the 6, put the 4 of hearts on it, send the 3 and 4 of hearts up, then send the 5 back.
