# Golf

Golf is a quick one-deck game: you clear seven face-up columns onto a single waste pile by playing cards one rank above or below the card showing. You can see every tableau card, so planning helps, but the order of the stock decides a lot, and with these rules wins are fairly rare.

## The deal

- Seven columns of five cards each, all face up (35 cards).
- The other 17 cards go face down to the stock.
- The waste starts empty.

The status line shows how many cards are left in the stock.

## Goal

Clear all 35 cards off the tableau onto the waste. The game is won as soon as the last column is empty, even if there are cards still in the stock.

## Moves

- The waste starts empty, so begin by turning a card from the stock.
- The top card of any column can go onto the waste if it is one rank above or below the waste's top card. Suit and colour don't matter, and a run can change direction as often as you like (6, 7, 8, 7, 6 is fine).
- Ranks don't wrap round. Only a 2 goes on an Ace, and nothing at all goes on a King, though a King can still go onto a Queen.
- Nothing else moves: cards never go from one column to another, and you can't play a card back off the waste.
- You can turn the next stock card onto the waste at any time, and you will need to whenever you have no play. The stock is turned one card at a time and there is no redeal.
- Click a playable column card, or press Enter or f on it, to send it to the waste.

## Scoring

You get one point for every card you play from the tableau to the waste, so a perfect game scores 35. Turning cards from the stock scores nothing. This matches AisleRiot.

## Options

Golf has no options.

## Tips

- Before you play, trace the longest chain you can find. Every card is in view, and a chain that takes six or seven cards off the tableau saves stock cards for later.
- When two cards could go on the waste, pick the one whose card underneath carries the chain on, or sets up a play in another column.
- Treat Kings as full stops. The only way a King leaves the tableau is onto a Queen, and after that you have to turn a stock card. So play a King when it would be your last move before turning anyway.
- Try not to let one column fall far behind the rest. A tall column with an awkward card buried deep is the usual reason a game ends two or three cards short.
