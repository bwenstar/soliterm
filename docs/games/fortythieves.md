# Forty Thieves

A two-deck game with ten columns that build down in suit and only one pass through the stock. It's a hard game: skill matters a great deal, but so does the order the stock comes out in, and many deals can't be won however you play them.

## The deal

Two full decks are shuffled together, 104 cards in all. Forty of them are dealt face up into ten columns of four. The other 64 go face down into the stock, with an empty waste beside it. The eight foundations start empty.

## Goal

Get all 104 cards onto the eight foundations. Each foundation holds one suit from Ace to King, so every suit ends up with two of them.

## Moves

- Columns build down in the same suit: the 9 of spades goes on the 10 of spades.
- You can move a run of one suit as a group, but only as a shortcut for moving it a card at a time through empty columns. With no empty columns you can move one card, and each empty column doubles that: 2 with one, 4 with two, 8 with three. If you're moving into an empty column, that column doesn't count.
- An empty column takes any card, or any run of one suit within that limit.
- Dealing turns one card from the stock onto the waste. Only the top card of the waste can be played, to a column or a foundation.
- There are no redeals. Once the stock is empty it stays empty.
- Foundations build up by suit from Ace to King. A whole run of one suit can go up in a single move if the card on top of it (its lowest card) is next on a foundation, however many empty columns you have.
- Cards on the foundations stay there. They can't be moved back.

Double-clicking a column or the waste (or pressing f on it) sends its top card to a foundation if it can go. If it can't, the card moves to the first column it builds on, or failing that to an empty column. Double-clicking a foundation sends up every card it can from the columns and the waste, and keeps going until nothing else will go. Autoplay is more careful: apart from Aces and twos, it holds a card back while the other copy of the card one rank below it is anywhere but a foundation (the stock included), since that copy may need it as a place to build.

## Scoring

Each card on a foundation is worth 5 points, and each foundation completed from Ace to King adds 60 more. A won game scores 1000: 520 for the cards and 480 for the eight finished suits. The score is worked out from the foundations after every move, and since foundation cards never come back it only goes up. This matches AisleRiot.

## Options

Forty Thieves has no options, so every deal is played by the rules above.

## Tips

- Go for an empty column early. It gives you a spare spot for any card and doubles the size of run you can move, and little else in this game helps as much.
- Make every useful move in the columns before you deal. Each card you turn up covers the one before it on the waste, and with no second pass the only way back to a buried card is to play everything on top of it.
- Try not to build a long run on top of a card you'll want soon. Moving the run off again takes empty columns: shifting a run of five onto another card needs three of them.
- There are two of every card. When a card could go on either copy of the card above it, pick the copy in the column you're less likely to need to dig into.
